Merchants who actually sell through catalog generate real incremental GPV — but the size depends on whether they already have payment hardware, and the direction of that dependence flips by channel. For SI, catalog helps merchants who don't already have hardware (+$3,179) much more than those who do (+$753, not significant) — hardware-equipped SI merchants are already capturing in-person sales, so catalog adds little on top. For SS, hardware ownership amplifies catalog's benefit (+$3,402 vs. +$1,566) — owning real hardware is rare and unusual for self-serve merchants, and likely marks an unusually committed, multi-channel operator who gets more out of every additional capability. In both channels, adding a product without ever completing a catalog sale is associated with lower GPV than never adding one at all — the value is in completing a catalog sale, not just setting one up.
Caveats: this is an adjusted observational comparison, not a randomized experiment — residual merchant-quality confounding can't be fully ruled out. The SI "no hardware" effect is borderline significant (p=0.051) on a smaller subgroup (n=3,460); the SS "has hardware" effect, while highly significant, is estimated on a rarer population (3.4% of SS) and shouldn't be read as typical of most self-serve merchants.
